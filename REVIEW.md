# Review – Aarhus Mobility 3D v2

Two review rounds were run on the finished v2 app, as asked: check the app, write down what can be better, fix it, and do it again.

## How it was checked

| Check | What it covers |
|---|---|
| `npm test` | 5 smoke tests (database, NetAScore import, demo data), 12 data tests (tag parsing, classification, multipolygons, pack conversion, roads, points, geodesy, starter pack, duplicate footprints) and 13 render tests (sun, local time, footprints, roofs, walls, courtyards, street ribbons, terrain normals, collisions, lamp light map, lit windows, sky model). |
| `test/e2e/run.js` | The real app (Electron main + renderer) runs under a virtual display with software WebGL (SwiftShader). It uses offline stand-ins for Overpass, Nominatim, terrain tiles and aerial photos. 22 scripted steps cover the following, and every screenshot was inspected by eye: <ul><li>boot and starter data</li><li>pack download through the Data window</li><li>classification statistics</li><li>map click</li><li>search</li><li>entering 3D</li><li>walking</li><li>collisions</li><li>inspecting with E</li><li>feedback with F</li><li>bike, drive and fly modes</li><li>street-level labels</li><li>dusk and night</li><li>night street lighting</li><li>rain</li><li>the NetAScore overlay</li><li>quality presets and toggles</li><li>frame statistics</li><li>back to the map</li><li>the activity log</li></ul> |

Software WebGL is slow, about 10 fps, so frame rates from the test only compare runs with each other. A desktop GPU is many times faster.

## Round 1 – findings and fixes

| # | Finding | How it showed up | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stuck inside terraced blocks.** Aarhus row houses share party walls. Pushing a player out of one footprint put them inside the neighbour, and the next frame pushed them back. Anyone who started or was teleported inside a block could not walk, bike or drive. | E2E: walk, bike and drive steps failed. The "walls block walking" step reported `pushedOut: false`. | `physics.js` was rewritten. A move that would end inside a footprint is refused. A point that is already inside searches outward, with 24 rays every 0.5 m, for the nearest spot clear of every building. Gaps narrower than the body never push you into a building. Chase cameras are clipped against walls. Cars and bikes scrape along walls at a glancing angle and bounce back only in a head-on hit. A new unit test covers all of these cases. |
| 2 | **"Building Aarhus around you…" never went away.** The loading card waited until no area anywhere was pending, including areas 2 km away. | E2E screenshots: the card stayed on screen during the whole visit. | The card hides as soon as every area within about 380 m has terrain and buildings, or after 25 s. Far areas keep streaming in behind the scenes. |
| 3 | **Night was a cream-coloured fog.** Three causes added up: <ul><li>Additive "light pool" decals, 12 m wide at 85 % opacity and overlapping, painted every street cream.</li><li>Bloom with a wide radius turned the bright windows into a veil over the whole screen.</li><li>Windows glowed at 2.6× intensity, with 48 % of them lit at any hour.</li></ul> | Night screenshots. | New **street-lamp light map** (`lamps.js`). Every lamp head is splatted into a camera-centred map using a cos³ fall-off. Ground, street and facade shaders then add *albedo × lamp light*, so dark asphalt gets a modest pool and pale paving a brighter one. Only walls that face a lamp are lit, and only up to about 10 m. Wet streets get a glossy streak. Bloom is calibrated (see 5). Windows: <ul><li>The share of lit windows follows the hour: about 46 % in the evening, 6 % at 4 am.</li><li>Light colours are warm incandescent, neutral LED or the blue of a screen.</li><li>Rooms are brighter at the ceiling, some curtains are drawn, and shop windows are lit.</li></ul> |
| 4 | **Washed-out daytime facades.** The sky's reflection map still contained the sun disc, with a radiance of about 60 000. It lit every surface a second time on top of the sun light. The reflection map also ran at full strength *and* a hemisphere light was added on top. | Daytime screenshots: pinkish-white sunlit walls with no contrast. | The reflection and ambient copy of the sky is drawn without the sun disc. Ambient strength comes from a CPU copy of the sky model (`skymodel.js`, see 5). Exposure is metered from the actual light. |
| 5 | **Rainy and overcast days glowed white.** Three.js `Sky.js` draws a thick cloud deck 5–10× brighter than a clear blue sky, as the sky-model test documents, and the reflection strength had even been *raised* for overcast weather. | Rain screenshot: pale peach walls with glowing window frames. | `skymodel.js` evaluates the same radiance formulas on the CPU. It calibrates: <ul><li>the strength of the sky light against realistic daylight for the weather and sun height;</li><li>a new `skyGain` patch, so heavy cloud is drawn at the brightness that actually lights the streets (what you see is what lights the scene);</li><li>a metered exposure;</li><li>a bloom threshold above the sky's own brightness.</li></ul> |
| 6 | **Haze did not match the sky.** Fog used one fixed grey-blue per weather, so distant buildings faded into the wrong colour. | Fly screenshot. | The haze is the sky's own colour near the horizon in the direction you look, averaged across the view and capped so a low sun cannot whiten everything. |
| 7 | **Trees and street furniture could not be inspected.** The picking filter `!material.blending` was false for every normal material, because `NormalBlending` is 1. | Code review while removing the decals. | Fixed. Crosshair and E now identify trees (species if mapped) and furniture. |
| 8 | **Street lighting gaps.** <ul><li>Lights vanished when furniture models were switched off.</li><li>A single mapped lamp in an area switched off all illustrative lamps there, so most streets went dark.</li></ul> | Night screenshot with a dark street. | Lamps light the streets even with furniture models hidden. Illustrative lamps now fill only the gaps: none within 18 m of a mapped lamp, and none inside buildings. |
| 9 | **Keys did not reach the 3D view** when the search box still had focus. | E2E keyboard step. | Entering 3D removes focus from text fields and focuses the 3D view. |
| 10 | **Spawning in narrow streets** could land on a sidewalk that is inside a building, or escape into a backyard. | Code review after fix 1. | The app tries the sidewalk on each side, then the street centre, and takes the first spot clear of buildings. If the buildings were still loading, the spot is checked again once they appear. When a car has to start in the left lane, it is turned so it drives on the right. |
| 11 | **Flickering twin walls.** GeoDanmark (and sometimes OSM) holds the same building twice, with two versions and different ids. That gives coplanar walls that z-fight, in 3D and on the map. | Night screenshot: a stippled window pattern. | `shared/dedupe.js` merges near-identical footprints (centroids under 1 m apart, areas within 25 %). It keeps the better-described twin and copies over any facts only the other one had. This runs when a pack is built and when any pack is loaded; it merged 12 pairs in the starter data. Unit-tested. |
| 12 | **Glass and asphalt.** <ul><li>Window glass was 35 % metallic, but glass is a dielectric.</li><li>Asphalt albedo 0.05 picked up a teal tint from sky reflections.</li><li>Car paint was very metallic.</li></ul> | Screenshots. | Glass is a smooth dielectric (Fresnel reflections of the sky). Aged city asphalt has albedo of about 0.08. Car paint is clear-coated, with lower metalness. |
| 13 | **Ambient occlusion darkened lamp-lit and lit-window areas at night.** | Night screenshots. | AO strength follows the share of ambient light, so it is weaker at night. |
| 14 | **No blue hour.** `Sky.js` goes black once the sun is 2° below the horizon, so 16:24 in November already had a starry night sky. | Dusk screenshot. | A twilight term was added: deep blue overhead and an orange band towards the set sun until the end of nautical twilight. The same term is in the CPU model, so haze and lighting match. Stars only appear after nautical dusk, and fewer of them because of city light. |
| 15 | **Overlapping labels.** | Street-level screenshots. | Labels are decluttered on screen: a less important label waits until there is room. The crosshair card keeps its own space. Labels are also rendered from their own small scene instead of the whole city scene graph. |
| 16 | **Slow ray tests.** The crosshair test runs 8 times a second and label occlusion fires up to 34 rays every 0.3 s, both against merged chunk meshes with tens of thousands of triangles. | Code review (frame-time spikes). | `three-mesh-bvh` (MIT) builds a bounding-volume hierarchy the first time a big mesh is ray-tested. Each test now takes microseconds instead of milliseconds. |
| 17 | **Draw-call statistics counted only the last post-processing pass.** | E2E statistics (1 call per frame). | `renderer.info` now counts the whole frame. |
| 18 | **The NetAScore overlay was hard to read**, especially in shade. | NetAScore screenshot. | The overlay colours are brighter, with a faint glow so they stay readable in shade and at night. |
| 19 | **Test-harness problems.** <ul><li>The test used the real clock, so 17:31 in November gave night shots.</li><li>A call returned an object that could not be cloned.</li><li>The campus fixture was outside the downloaded area.</li><li>Screenshots were one step late, because software frames are slow and hidden windows throttle `requestAnimationFrame`.</li><li>Key presses were timed in wall-clock time.</li></ul> | E2E report. | Daytime is set explicitly. Screenshots wait for rendered frames. Keys are held for *simulated* seconds. The window is never throttled. The campus fixture lies inside the area, and 171 buildings are now classified as university buildings by context. |

**Round 1 result:** 20 of 22 end-to-end steps passed with 0 console errors (before: 16/21). The bike and drive steps still failed. Round 2 found the real cause of the bike failure (finding 21).

## Round 2 – findings and fixes

The whole app was checked again: the end-to-end run, every screenshot, and a code read of the streets, buildings, labels, input and robustness.

| # | Finding | How it showed up | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | **Sidewalks, cycle tracks and curbs ran straight across junctions.** They were drawn along the whole street, so at every crossing pale slab bands and little curb walls lay across the other street's carriageway. That is not acceptable for "precise streets". | Code read of the street builder. | Each junction node now knows the width of the motor streets that meet there (`worlddata.crossWidth`). The side ribbons and their curbs are cut exactly at the curb line of the crossing street, using exact polyline slices (`slicePolyline`, `keepIntervals`). Illustrative lamps are never placed inside a crossing. Unit-tested. |
| 21 | **Keys did nothing after leaving feedback.** After submitting a comment and closing the panel, the comment box still had keyboard focus, so `2` (bike) and `R` were swallowed. This was the real reason the bike step failed. | E2E bike step, now with a diagnostic that reads the mode and the focused element. | Closing the info panel gives the keyboard back to the 3D view. `Esc` closes the panel from anywhere, including inside the comment box. The E2E now reports `focusAfterPanel: "CANVAS"` and bike mode reaches 14.6 km/h. |
| 22 | **Stippled lit windows.** Some lit windows showed a random dot pattern. The building's seed reaches the shader as an interpolated vertex attribute carrying about 1e-7 of noise. The hash functions that choose lit / colour / curtains amplify that noise, so a window whose hash lies near a threshold flipped from pixel to pixel. | Night screenshot, zoomed. A diagnostic run ray-traced the pixel and found **one** surface (so not z-fighting), and an A/B build with curtains disabled made the dots vanish. | The seed is snapped back in the shader to the exact value set on the CPU, so every pixel of a window makes the same choice. Re-verified with the same diagnostic run. |
| 22b | **Overlapping footprints can z-fight.** A block outline with the houses inside it, for example, gives coplanar walls of different buildings. De-duplication (11) only catches near-identical twins; the test data has 2 such overlapping pairs within 45 m of one street. | Overlap analysis of the downloaded pack. | Every building's walls get a building-specific 0.5–5 cm outward offset (mitred corners; courtyards shrink accordingly), so two buildings never share a wall plane. Unit-tested. |
| 22c | **Two `smoothstep` calls had reversed edges** (`smoothstep(11.0, 1.5, x)`). That is undefined behaviour in GLSL ES and can differ between GPUs and ANGLE back-ends (D3D on Windows). | Code read while chasing 22. | Rewritten as `1.0 - smoothstep(low, high, x)`. |
| 23 | **Labels near you were hidden.** A label floats above the middle of its roof, so from the street the line of sight hits the building's own facade first. The occlusion test treated that as hidden. | Street-level screenshot with no labels after the declutter change. A synthetic test reproduced it identically with and without the BVH. | A label is hidden only by *another* building. Hits on its own building, or on the building a shop or café is in, are ignored. |
| 24 | **Night-time stutter.** Splatting the lamp light map cost 30–60 ms on the main thread (per-texel square roots and powers on 512² texels). That caused a hitch each time the map re-centred while you moved. | Benchmark. | A precomputed lamp stamp on a 2 m grid (320², still smooth thanks to bilinear filtering). The median is now 3 ms, and 0.7 ms once warmed up. |
| 25 | **No street lighting switch.** | Settings review. | ⚙️ → *Street lighting at night*. |
| 26 | **A GPU reset would leave a black 3D view.** This happens after driver updates, sleep, or switching GPUs on laptops. | Robustness review. | The WebGL context is kept restorable. After a restore the sky reflections and post-processing are rebuilt and the lamp map is re-uploaded. |
| 27 | **Traffic at night was invisible.** Cars, cyclists and trams were dark shapes. | Night screenshot. | Head- and taillights on all traffic vehicles. Cars and trams have lights on, dimmed by day (Danish daytime running lights) and full after dark. Bikes have front and rear lights. |
| 28 | **The end-to-end test counted some failures as passes.** A step that returned an object with `error` or `ok: false` was counted as passed. | Test-harness review. | Such steps now count as failures. |
| 29 | **The spawn re-check could pull a walking player back.** Fix 10 re-checks the spot once the buildings around you have loaded. On a slow machine you may already be walking by then, and walking along a facade (closer than 45 cm) counted as "not free", so you were put back at the start. | E2E run while the exe was being built on the same CPU, so chunks loaded slower than usual. | The re-check only acts if a building really appeared *around* you (you are inside its footprint). |

**Round 2 result:** **22 of 22 end-to-end steps pass, with 0 console errors and 0 fatal errors.** All 32 unit tests pass.

## What could still be better

These are known limitations, written down honestly rather than hidden:

- **Building interiors and detailed 3D landmarks** (the cathedral spire, ARoS's rainbow, Dokk1) are generic massing from footprints and heights. Real landmark models would have to be modelled or imported (glTF) one by one.
- **Corners of sidewalks** at junctions are square, not rounded curbs. Pedestrian crossings appear only where OSM maps them.
- **Facades are procedural** (brick, plaster, panels, windows by floor) rather than photographed. Open street-level photos (Mapillary) would need a large photogrammetry pipeline before they could be used as textures.
- **Traffic** is illustrative: cars follow the street graph and stop for you, but there are no traffic-light phases.
- **The software-rendered test** reaches about 10 fps. Real GPUs are much faster, but very old integrated GPUs should use the *Low* preset.
